# Implementation notes (2026-10-07)

Choices made while building Release 1 where the brief or the planning documents left room. None changes a design principle of brief 1.1. The product owner delegated these to the implementation (2026-10-07: "You make those decisions"), so they stand as decided. Each is easy to change if counsel or a customer says otherwise.

## Decided

| # | Choice | Why | Where |
|---|--------|-----|-------|
| 1 | Only **Priority** and **Potential** candidates can move from "screened" to "contacting". Hold and Not Qualified cannot. | Planning handoff section 3 says only those two continue to outreach. | `pipeline/service.ts`, `screening/service.ts` |
| 2 | A **blind resume** leaves out the name and contact details. Employers, titles and dates stay. | Changing them would break "never alter resume facts" (brief 9.1). A stricter blind resume (employers hidden) needs a decision. | `ai/prompts/resume-tailor.ts` |
| 3 | The tailored resume's **facts are copied by code** from the confirmed profile (employers, titles, dates, degrees); the model only writes the summary, the bullets and the skill order. | Makes "do not change or exaggerate facts" structural, on top of the fact check. | `resume-tailor.ts` |
| 4 | The first interview round added to a **submitted** candidate moves them to "interviewing"; a round added to a candidate on hold puts them back in play. | Adding a round is already the recruiter's explicit act. | `interviews/service.ts` |
| 5 | When accepted placements reach the position's openings the position becomes **filled**; if one falls through (declined after acceptance, reneged) it goes back to **interviewing**. | Brief 12 and FR-OFF-03 say "filled when openings are met" but not what happens afterwards. | `offers/service.ts` |
| 6 | Offer **reminders**: one open task at a time (the day after the resignation date, a week before the start, the day after the start); completing one schedules the next. | FR-OFF-02 names the moments; the one-open-task rule of the task engine decides the mechanism. | `offers/reminders.ts` |
| 7 | **Privacy requests** get a 45-day deadline (the longest common U.S. state period) and a reminder a week before. Leads and admins record them; only an admin carries them out. | FR-PLT-08 asks for deadline tracking, not the period. Korea's PIPA is shorter (10 days). | `core/schemas/privacy.ts` |
| 8 | **Deletion** removes files, artifacts (all versions), documents, match analyses and embeddings of the candidate and redacts personal text in audit rows (activity details, approver names, free-text reasons, messages to the candidate, interview comments). Pipeline transitions and stages stay. A stored original that another candidate also uses is kept. | ADR-016, plus a decision on which free-text fields count as personal. | `privacy/anonymize.ts` |
| 9 | The opt-out link in an outreach email **suppresses the address and marks every matching candidate do-not-contact**. | FR-MSG-05 and FR-SRC-09 link them; marking the candidate also blocks drafting. | `messaging/unsubscribe.ts` |
| 10 | Outreach and report drafts use **placeholders** for names (`{{first_name}}`, `{{recruiter_name}}`) filled by code at preview, copy and send. The report text says "the candidate"; the PDF header shows the name. | Keeps the candidate's name away from the model (FR-AI-09). | `outreach/service.ts`, `ai/prompts/report-draft.ts` |
| 11 | A submission needs an **up-to-date PDF of both the report and the resume**, and pins those exact files. Submitting the same candidate to the same client again asks for a reason. | US-802 and FR-SUB-04. | `submission/submit.ts` |
| 12 | The candidate's **approval of the resume is bound to the exact approved version**: approving a changed version creates a new record that has no approval yet. | FR-RES-03: an approval must not cover text the candidate did not see. | `artifacts/hooks.ts`, `resume/approval.ts` |
| 13 | The text **Voyage** receives for a profile is the approved profile without name, email, phone, links and the AI-inferred "potential" fields; the candidate's name words are also cut from free text, and protected characteristics are redacted as for every other AI input. | FR-AI-09 and ADR-008: Voyage is a sub-processor of candidate data, not of identity. | `packages/core/src/candidates/embedding-text.ts`, `server/embeddings/indexing.ts` |
| 14 | **Indexing** is queued by the approval hook (`indexing:embed`), keeps one vector per subject (a newer approval replaces the older), skips candidates who are not active, stops for good on a missing key or a used-up budget, and a **nightly catch-up** (04:00 UTC) queues approved profiles that have no vector. | ADR-008 names indexing as the one automatic AI step; a missed job must not leave a profile unsearchable. | `server/embeddings/*`, `worker/scheduler.ts` |
| 15 | "Find in my pool" embeds the success profile **at search time** (query type, metered, charged to the position) instead of using its stored vector; lists up to 25, **leaves out only people already in the position and discarded or merged records**, shows do-not-contact people flagged, and says how many were not indexed. If Voyage cannot be used it falls back to a **keyword search and says so**. The result is stored as a draft `similar_candidates` artifact; it needs no approval because adding a person is the recruiter's own click. | Brief 1.1: score is a reference, nobody is hidden. Query-type vectors retrieve better, and a stored success-profile vector would go stale. | `server/pool/similar.ts` |
| 16 | "Check open positions": **open** means intake, sourcing or interviewing; the 30 newest positions the person may see are considered; positions with no confirmed success profile or that already hold the candidate are listed under **Not assessed** with the reason; Low fits are listed. | Brief 4.3 shows Strong to Low in one list; the brief does not define "open". | `server/pool/portfolio.ts` |
| 17 | **Benchmark, web part:** the Claude web search tool (`web_search_20260209`), at most 5 searches, known paid providers blocked; a web finding is kept only if it carries the label of a page the search actually cited, links and dates come from the search and not from the model, and findings on paid sources are dropped and counted. The web part is **off until `WEB_SEARCH=on`**. Each search is billed at $0.01 in the ledger. | Q-05 (no paid data), ADR-005 (two calls because citations cannot be combined with structured output). | `ai/prompts/market-benchmark.ts`, `ai/anthropic.ts` |
| 18 | **Benchmark, "Our data":** counted by code from the positions the person may see whose title matches (level words ignored); every line links to its positions. No model writes it. | Q-21: labelled apart and traceable; a model must not restate the agency's own numbers. | `server/benchmark/own-data.ts` |
| 19 | **Suggested JD changes** (add a requirement, change one, update a section) name the findings they rest on, can be decided only on the **approved** report, and an accepted one is written into a **new draft of the structured JD**, never into an approved one. One decision per suggestion; no undo. | Brief 3.3 and 1.1: the recruiter reviews and approves the improved JD. | `server/benchmark/service.ts` |
| 20 | The Voyage key lives next to the Anthropic key (`/admin/settings`, encrypted, environment as fallback) and is **no longer required in production** from the environment. `benchmark_report`, `similar_candidates` and `portfolio_fit` now have real schemas; two older tests that used `benchmark_report` as a placeholder use `improved_jd`. | Handoff rule for keys. | `server/platform/secrets.ts`, `lib/env.ts` |
| 21 | **Analytics cohort:** every rate KPI, the stage funnel and the source table are computed over the pipeline entries *added to a position* in the selected dates (both ends inclusive, UTC days; default the last 90 days). Time-to-Fill is the exception: it covers positions *filled* in the dates. Time-to-Interview covers entries in the cohort. | planning-handoff section 6 gives the formulas but not which records a date range selects. A cohort makes "last quarter's candidates" comparable and keeps ratios between 0 and 100%. | `analytics/kpis.ts` |
| 22 | An entry has **reached** a stage when it has a transition into that stage or a later one, so an overridden guard that skips a step does not break the funnel. A sent touch counts as contacted and a held interview as interviewing. "Has an interview" means at least one interview that was not cancelled. A recorded "no response" is not a reply. | The state machine allows skips (guard overrides) and the formulas count "reaching" a stage. | `analytics/kpis.ts` |
| 23 | **Who sees what:** every member can open Analytics. A recruiter always gets their own figures (the recruiter filter is ignored for them); a lead or admin sees the agency and may pick one recruiter. | Brief section 13: the recruiter checks "their own" performance; US-1101 says the recruiter filter is for leads. | `analytics/service.ts` |
| 24 | **Capacity:** positions per recruiter is the average, over the weeks of the range, of positions in sourcing, interviewing or offer (on hold, intake and draft excluded) at the end of each week, by the position's current owner. Time per position is the median of *active days* (days in those statuses) of positions filled in the range. A rate with no denominator shows "No data yet", never 0%. CSV cells starting with `= + - @` are prefixed so spreadsheets do not run them. | DP-5 asks for positions per recruiter and time per position; the handoff allows "active days" as the system proxy for recruiter hours. | `analytics/capacity.ts`, `analytics/csv.ts` |
| 25 | **Portal notices:** a portal user gets a short email when something is shared with them, when a shared item is replaced ("updated"), and when the recruiter answers their recommendation. The email says what kind of thing is waiting and links to the portal home; it has no content, no names and no sign-in token (they sign in again by emailed link if the session ended). The email is queued in the same transaction as the change and dispatched after it commits. A person who was turned off gets nothing. | US-1404 asks that the HM finds submissions without digging through email, not that the content travels by email; keeping content out limits what a misdirected message leaks. | `portal/notify.ts`, `messaging/templates.ts` |
| 26 | **Candidate status share:** the agency chooses `off`, `basic` (position and a general step) or `detailed` (adds the client, interview count and date of the last change); default `basic`. Only applications that were put forward to a client are listed. The candidate never sees client feedback, rejection reasons, scores or other candidates. A recruiter shares it once; after that the snapshot is refreshed in place on every pipeline move and interview change (no email each time), and withdrawn if the agency turns the level to `off`. | FR-CDP-05 asks for "a level the agency chooses". Refreshing in place keeps one share instead of a pile of copies; the recruiter's act of sharing covers the updates. | `portal/status-snapshot.ts`, `pipeline/service.ts` |
| 27 | **Portal adoption** = approvals (JD and tailored resume) + submission reviews + interview feedback done in a portal ÷ the same three kinds done by any route. A submission review is a hiring manager's answer to a submitted candidate: it counts as a portal review when the hiring manager proposed the decision in the portal, otherwise as recorded by the recruiter. | planning-handoff §6 defines it in one line. | `analytics/adoption.ts` |
| 28 | **A17 (feedback from a pasted email):** the email goes to the model without the candidate's name, email addresses and phone numbers (the same scrubbing now applies to A14 call notes). The model rates a criterion only when the text says how the person did on it; otherwise it is left empty. The draft is an `interview_feedback` artifact per round; **approving it is the recruiter's confirmation**, and it is refused until every rating that was given is ticked as confirmed. Approval records the feedback once per version (`InterviewFeedback.artifactVersionId`, new migration). | FR-IVW-04: "recruiter confirms each rating". One record per approved version keeps a re-approval from duplicating feedback. | `ai/prompts/feedback-structure.ts`, `interviews/feedback-draft.ts`, `artifacts/hooks.ts` |
| 29 | **Re-analysis (T-3-12):** an out-of-date analysis shows an "Analyze again" button on the analysis page, on its row of the dashboard, and as "Analyze out-of-date again (n)" for all of them (the owner, a lead or an admin; up to 50 at a time). A new analysis is a new unreviewed draft: the "reviewed" mark never carries over, so the recruiter reads it again before it feeds a call or a report. | FR-MAT-07 asks for the marker and a one-click refresh; the review rule is the brief's "AI output is a draft until approved". | `review-board.tsx`, `analysis-view.tsx` |
| 30 | **Tags and notes (T-2-13):** tags are short lowercase labels (at most 20, 40 characters each, no commas) that any member can set; the pool filters by them and a tag on the candidate page links to that filter. Notes are separate rows with author and time; anyone in the agency reads them, only the author edits, the author or an admin deletes. The timeline records that a note was added, never its text. Notes are in the candidate export and are deleted with the candidate (they are personal data). | FR-SRC-09 names tags and notes but not who may do what; notes can hold sensitive remarks, so the timeline keeps only the fact. | `candidates/notes.ts`, `CandidateNote` (new table, RLS) |
| 31 | **Bulk resume upload (T-2-14):** up to 20 files per go, one source for all, three uploads at a time, each going through the same intake as a single file (a draft candidate, text read in the background, AI proposes the profile). The page follows each draft (reading, AI writing, ready, confirmed) with a link to review; nothing becomes a candidate until the recruiter confirms. Files that are not PDF, DOCX or text, or over 10 MB, are refused in the list and the others continue. | FR-SRC-10; uploading is the recruiter's request for the AI step (FR-AI-01), so no AI runs unasked. | `components/candidates/bulk-upload.tsx` |
| 32 | **CSV import (T-2-15, Q-10):** the file (up to 2 MB and 2,000 rows) is read in the browser; the recruiter maps columns (guessed from English and Korean headings; first and last name are joined as "first last"); rows go in batches of 100. Each row becomes an **active** candidate whose profile is written by the recruiter (origin human, approved in the same step) from only what the file says, so lists, search and duplicate checks work; matching analysis still needs a fuller profile from a resume. A row that matches an existing candidate (email, phone, profile link, or a similar name with the same employer) is **skipped and listed, never merged or overwritten**. An address on the opt-out list is imported as do-not-contact. A notes column becomes a note by the importer. | Q-10's proposed answer is a one-time CSV import. Skipping rather than merging keeps import from silently changing existing records. | `candidates/import-csv.ts`, `components/candidates/csv-import.tsx` |
| 33 | **Outreach templates (T-5-11):** a template is a recruiter's own (private to them) or the agency's (shared by a lead or admin), for one channel and optionally one client. The composer lists the ones that fit the touch's channel and the position's client; picking one makes the draft start from it: the AI keeps its structure, wording, greeting and sign-off, adds no claim that is neither in the template nor in the facts, and keeps the placeholders. Prompt version is now `outreach-draft-v2`; the template used is recorded with the draft's inputs. A recruiter edits only their own templates; leads and admins edit the shared ones. | FR-OUT-06 says "per recruiter and per client". Keeping personal templates private avoids one recruiter's wording being edited or read by another. | `outreach/templates.ts`, `ai/prompts/outreach-draft.ts` |
| 34 | **Email transports (T-5-02):** an agency picks one of four in Settings, Defaults: test mode (default), test mode with a log line, SMTP, or each recruiter's connected mailbox. SMTP uses one platform-wide service (`SMTP_*`, `MAIL_FROM`); the visible sender is `MAIL_FROM` named "Recruiter (Agency)" and **Reply-To is the recruiter**, so the conversation continues in their inbox. Picking SMTP is refused while it is not configured, and a transport that cannot work **fails each send at once and says why** instead of quietly capturing it. A rejection by the server (5xx) or a refused login is permanent (no retries); everything else retries up to 5 times. nodemailer (MIT-0) is the only new dependency. | ADR-009 names nodemailer. Silent fallback to capture would let a recruiter believe a message went out. | `messaging/smtp.ts`, `messaging/transport.ts`, `messaging/send.ts` |
| 35 | **Connected mailboxes and reply sync (T-5-12):** one mailbox per recruiter (Gmail or Microsoft 365), connected through Interactor's credential service; only the credential id and the address are kept. Replies are found by **polling the provider every 10 minutes** for conversations of messages sent in the last 30 days (Interactor documents no mail-reply webhook); only sender, time and conversation are read, never message text. A reply marks the touch `replied`, cancels the rest of the sequence and creates a "Reply to read" task; **the candidate is not moved**: the recruiter classifies the reply, which is the step that can move them (brief section 1.1). A mailbox whose sign-in stops working is marked "connect again" and its sends fail at once with that message. The Interactor service ids and scopes are in `docs/planning/mail-setup.md`; the defaults are guesses to confirm. | FR-MSG-04, US-505, ADR-009. Marking "replied" is a fact the system saw; its meaning is the recruiter's call. | `messaging/mailbox.ts`, `messaging/reply-sync.ts`, `outreach/replies.ts`, `lib/interactor/credentials.ts` |

## Running things

- **Database roles:** `pnpm db:setup` now also creates `easemuse_datarights`, the only login that can change audit rows. The worker needs `DATABASE_URL_DATARIGHTS` for candidate deletion (see `.env.example`); the job refuses to run without it.
- **AI evaluation:** `pnpm eval -- --suite all --max-cost 15` runs the labelled data in `eval-data/` against the real model (needs `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`; see `apps/web/evals/README.md`). Nothing has been run against a live model yet.
- **Print pages in a real browser:** see the header of `src/server/documents/render/live-render.db.test.ts`. The JD, resume and report pages have been printed with Chrome against a dev server and look right.
- **Not built:** SMTP transport (postponed on request), Korean translations (skeleton only, per ADR-015), a logo on documents, a Playwright happy path (the loop is covered by `src/server/happy-path.db.test.ts` at service level, and axe runs on the screens at component level).
- **Keys for pool search and the benchmark (2026-10-07):** the Voyage key (`pa-...`) is entered by an owner in `/admin/settings` or set as `VOYAGE_API_KEY`; without it pool search uses keywords and new profiles are not indexed (the nightly catch-up indexes them once a key exists). Web search needs `WEB_SEARCH=on` **and** web search switched on for the Anthropic account in the Claude Console; the key also needs credit. Nothing in this area has been run against a live provider: Voyage and web search are covered by fakes, and pgvector by the real database.
