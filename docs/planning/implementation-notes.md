# Implementation notes (2026-10-07)

Choices made while building Release 1 where the brief or the planning documents left room. None changes a design principle of brief 1.1. The product owner delegated these to the implementation (2026-10-07: "You make those decisions"), so they stand as decided. Each is easy to change if counsel or a customer says otherwise.

## Decided

| # | Choice | Why | Where |
|---|--------|-----|-------|
| 1 | Only **Priority** and **Potential** candidates can move from "screened" to "contacting". Hold and Not Qualified cannot. | Planning handoff section 3 says only those two continue to outreach. | `pipeline/service.ts`, `screening/service.ts` |
| 2 | A **blind resume** leaves out the name and contact details. Employers, titles and dates stay. | Changing them would break "never alter resume facts" (brief 9.1). A stricter blind resume (employers hidden) needs a decision. | `ai/prompts/resume-tailor.ts` |
| 3 | The tailored resume's **facts are copied by code** from the confirmed profile (employers, titles, dates, degrees); the model only writes the summary, the bullets and the skill order. | Makes "do not change or exaggerate facts" structural, on top of the fact check. | `resume-tailor.ts` |
| 4 | The first interview round added to a **submitted** candidate moves them to "interviewing"; a round added to a candidate on hold puts them back in play. | Adding a round is already the recruiter's explicit act. | `interviews/service.ts` |
| 5 | When accepted placements reach the position's openings the position becomes **filled**; if one falls through (declined after acceptance, reneged) it goes back to **interviewing**. | Brief 12 and FR-OFF-03 say "filled when openings are met" but not what happens afterwards. | `offers/service.ts` |
| 6 | Offer **reminders**: one open task at a time (the day after the resignation date, a week before the start, the day after the start); completing one schedules the next. | FR-OFF-02 names the moments; the one-open-task rule of the task engine decides the mechanism. | `offers/reminders.ts` |
| 7 | **Privacy requests** get a 45-day deadline (the longest common U.S. state period) and a reminder a week before. Leads and admins record them; only an admin carries them out. | FR-PLT-08 asks for deadline tracking, not the period. Korea's PIPA is shorter (10 days). | `core/schemas/privacy.ts` |
| 8 | **Deletion** removes files, artifacts (all versions), documents, match analyses and embeddings of the candidate and redacts personal text in audit rows (activity details, approver names, free-text reasons, messages to the candidate, interview comments). Pipeline transitions and stages stay. A stored original that another candidate also uses is kept. | ADR-016, plus a decision on which free-text fields count as personal. | `privacy/anonymize.ts` |
| 9 | The opt-out link in an outreach email **suppresses the address and marks every matching candidate do-not-contact**. | FR-MSG-05 and FR-SRC-09 link them; marking the candidate also blocks drafting. | `messaging/unsubscribe.ts` |
| 10 | Outreach and report drafts use **placeholders** for names (`{{first_name}}`, `{{recruiter_name}}`) filled by code at preview, copy and send. The report text says "the candidate"; the PDF header shows the name. | Keeps the candidate's name away from the model (FR-AI-09). | `outreach/service.ts`, `ai/prompts/report-draft.ts` |
| 11 | A submission needs an **up-to-date PDF of both the report and the resume**, and pins those exact files. Submitting the same candidate to the same client again asks for a reason. | US-802 and FR-SUB-04. | `submission/submit.ts` |
| 12 | The candidate's **approval of the resume is bound to the exact approved version**: approving a changed version creates a new record that has no approval yet. | FR-RES-03: an approval must not cover text the candidate did not see. | `artifacts/hooks.ts`, `resume/approval.ts` |
| 13 | The text **Voyage** receives for a profile is the approved profile without name, email, phone, links and the AI-inferred "potential" fields; the candidate's name words are also cut from free text, and protected characteristics are redacted as for every other AI input. | FR-AI-09 and ADR-008: Voyage is a sub-processor of candidate data, not of identity. | `packages/core/src/candidates/embedding-text.ts`, `server/embeddings/indexing.ts` |
| 14 | **Indexing** is queued by the approval hook (`indexing:embed`), keeps one vector per subject (a newer approval replaces the older), skips candidates who are not active, stops for good on a missing key or a used-up budget, and a **nightly catch-up** (04:00 UTC) queues approved profiles that have no vector. | ADR-008 names indexing as the one automatic AI step; a missed job must not leave a profile unsearchable. | `server/embeddings/*`, `worker/scheduler.ts` |
| 15 | "Find in my pool" embeds the success profile **at search time** (query type, metered, charged to the position) instead of using its stored vector; lists up to 25, **leaves out only people already in the position and discarded or merged records**, shows do-not-contact people flagged, and says how many were not indexed. If Voyage cannot be used it falls back to a **keyword search and says so**. The result is stored as a draft `similar_candidates` artifact; it needs no approval because adding a person is the recruiter's own click. | Brief 1.1: score is a reference, nobody is hidden. Query-type vectors retrieve better, and a stored success-profile vector would go stale. | `server/pool/similar.ts` |
| 16 | "Check open positions": **open** means intake, sourcing or interviewing; the 30 newest positions the person may see are considered; positions with no confirmed success profile or that already hold the candidate are listed under **Not assessed** with the reason; Low fits are listed. | Brief 4.3 shows Strong to Low in one list; the brief does not define "open". | `server/pool/portfolio.ts` |
| 17 | **Benchmark, web part:** the Claude web search tool (`web_search_20260209`), at most 5 searches, known paid providers blocked; a web finding is kept only if it carries the label of a page the search actually cited, links and dates come from the search and not from the model, and findings on paid sources are dropped and counted. The web part is **off until `WEB_SEARCH=on`**. Each search is billed at $0.01 in the ledger. | Q-05 (no paid data), ADR-005 (two calls because citations cannot be combined with structured output). | `ai/prompts/market-benchmark.ts`, `ai/anthropic.ts` |
| 18 | **Benchmark, "Our data":** counted by code from the positions the person may see whose title matches (level words ignored); every line links to its positions. No model writes it. | Q-21: labelled apart and traceable; a model must not restate the agency's own numbers. | `server/benchmark/own-data.ts` |
| 19 | **Suggested JD changes** (add a requirement, change one, update a section) name the findings they rest on, can be decided only on the **approved** report, and an accepted one is written into a **new draft of the structured JD**, never into an approved one. One decision per suggestion; no undo. | Brief 3.3 and 1.1: the recruiter reviews and approves the improved JD. | `server/benchmark/service.ts` |
| 20 | The Voyage key lives next to the Anthropic key (`/admin/settings`, encrypted, environment as fallback) and is **no longer required in production** from the environment. `benchmark_report`, `similar_candidates` and `portfolio_fit` now have real schemas; two older tests that used `benchmark_report` as a placeholder use `improved_jd`. | Handoff rule for keys. | `server/platform/secrets.ts`, `lib/env.ts` |
| 21 | **Analytics rates follow a cohort:** an entry counts in the denominator when its starting event falls in the period (first outreach sent, submitted, first interview, offer, added) and in the numerator if it reached the next step at any time. A step skipped on the way counts as reached, and a "no" at a step proves that step (not interested = contacted, declined = offer, reneged = accepted); rejected, on hold and withdrawn prove nothing. | Planning-handoff 6 gives the ratios, not how a period applies to them. | `server/analytics/compute.ts` |
| 22 | **Times:** time to fill runs from the position's first move to sourcing (else its creation) to its first accepted offer; time to interview from submission to the first interview's own date (the move to "interviewing" only when no interview was recorded; cancelled interviews ignored). Both are medians, counted when the closing event is in the period. **Capacity** counts a position in every week of the period in which it was in sourcing, interviewing or offer, per owning recruiter; time per position is sourcing to filled. | Same section; "time per position" uses the system proxy because there is no time tracking. | `server/analytics/compute.ts` |
| 23 | Analytics **respects the agency's position visibility rule** (a recruiter limited to their own positions sees only their numbers); the recruiter filter is offered to leads and admins. Default period: the last 90 days including today; at most two years. CSV export neutralises spreadsheet formulas in text cells. | ADR-012; no leak through aggregates. | `server/analytics/service.ts` |
| 24 | A **fake AI model** (`AI_FAKE=on`) answers every registered purpose with a deterministic draft built from the input, so the whole loop runs without a provider or credit. It goes through the same gateway, schemas, provenance and approval as the real model; it never fills a name or contact detail (the real model never sees them); web search reports itself unavailable. It is **refused in production** both at startup (`parseEnv`) and by the client factory. | E2E and local demos without spending credit; the same safety rules as the real path. | `server/ai/fake/`, `lib/env.ts`, `server/ai/index.ts` |
| 25 | The **E2E environment** is separate from development: port 3100, the `easemuse_test` database (refused unless its name ends in `_test`), Redis database 1, a test-only session secret, sessions minted as Auth.js cookies. Each run seeds its own users and agency and removes them afterwards. | Running E2E must never touch dev data or the dev worker's queue. | `apps/web/tests/e2e/support/`, `playwright.config.ts` |
| 26 | **Date inputs** send the chosen day as local noon, capped at now, and default to the user's local day. | A fixed UTC hour made "today" a future date for part of every day (found by the E2E run), and the server refuses future dates. | `lib/dates.ts` |
| 27 | **Branding** (FR-TEN-04, session B): a brand colour must reach a 4.5:1 contrast on white, and the logo must be a PNG or JPEG of at most 512 KB and 4000 px a side, checked from the file bytes. SVG is refused (scripts, and Word cannot draw it). | The colour is used as text and as a rule on white pages and documents. | `core/schemas/branding.ts`, `branding/service.ts` |
| 28 | The logo is stored in the agency's own database tables (`FileObject` and `FileBlob`, under RLS) and served without sign-in at `/api/public/agencies/:slug/logo`. A new upload replaces and deletes the old file. | Portal pages and emails need an address anyone can load; it is the same image the public portal sign-in page shows. It also means no object storage is needed for the logo, and tenant deletion and export see it like any other file. | `branding/service.ts`, `api/public/agencies/[agencySlug]/logo` |
| 29 | PDFs get the logo as inline image data (no second request), Word files embed it (at most 160 x 48 px, top right), and the render hash already covers the logo file id, so a new logo makes the stored PDFs out of date. Emails get a header with the logo (a link to the public route), the name and a rule in the colour; the call-to-action button takes the colour. | One source of branding for every output. | `components/documents/logo-mark.tsx`, `documents/logo-docx.ts`, `messaging/templates.ts` |
| 30 | **Support access** (T-13-07, FR-ADM-07, FR-TEN-07): a platform administrator of any role files a request (reason, 1 to 72 hours); only an agency admin can approve, deny or later revoke it. A request nobody answers lapses after 7 days. One open request or grant per person and agency. There is no email notice yet (SMTP is postponed): the admin sees the request under Settings > Support access. | The brief of the task says "an agency admin grants"; FR-ADM-07 says "request ... from an agency admin". Both are met by request then approval. | `support/access.ts` |
| 31 | While a grant is active the platform administrator signs in to `/a/<slug>` as a **lead who cannot administer** (sees every position, no admin pages) and may only read. A proxy (`src/proxy.ts`) stamps each request with its real method and path in headers the browser cannot set (checked in a running Next server: a request sent with forged `x-easemuse-*` headers arrives with the real ones); the authorizer refuses anything but GET or HEAD, and refuses when the method is unknown. Every read is logged in the platform audit log and the agency's activity log in one transaction **before** it is served; if logging fails, the request fails. A banner on every page says who, that it is read-only and until when. | Route handlers cannot see the method otherwise, and "fail closed" was the safer choice than "allow when unknown". | `lib/auth/authorizers.ts`, `proxy.ts` |
| 32 | **Tenant export** (T-13-09, FR-TEN-06): an agency admin asks for a ZIP; a worker job builds it from `TENANT_TABLES` (the same list the deletion uses): `data/<Table>.ndjson` (one JSON object per line), `files/<id>/<name>`, `agency.json`, `users.json` (the agency's members), `manifest.json` and a README that documents the format. One-time link hashes and search embeddings are left out; earlier exports are not packed into the next. It is built in memory, so it is capped at 512 MB and fails with a clear message beyond that; the last two exports are kept. | "Export in documented format". A streaming build is the next step if a tenant ever needs more than 512 MB. | `tenant/export.ts`, `tenant/tables.ts` |
| 33 | **Tenant deletion** (T-13-10, FR-ADM-08, ADR-016): a platform **owner or admin** (never `support`) asks, typing the agency's URL name; the agency must already be **suspended** (so nobody signs in and no job runs while it is removed). A **different** owner or admin approves, typing the name again. Either can cancel until it runs. Then a system job (its `JobRun` has no agency, so it outlives the tenant) runs as the data-rights role: files in object storage, then one `DELETE` per table in `TENANT_TABLES` order, then members who belong to no other agency, are not platform administrators and appear in no platform audit entry, then the agency row. Progress is saved after every table, so a retry carries on; the record is marked failed only after the third attempt. The platform audit log and the `TenantDeletion` record (ids, slug, name, who, when, row counts; no content) stay. | The brief of the task says "a second owner's approval"; FR-ADM-08 says "second owner/admin". The typed name at both steps is stricter than the requirement. Export is the agency admin's own tool and is not forced before deletion. | `tenant/deletion.ts`, `tenant/tables.ts` |
| 34 | **System health** (T-16-01, FR-ADM-09): per period (1 hour to 7 days, default 24 hours): per queue the waiting, running, done and failed runs and the oldest waiting run; per agency the failed jobs, failed and bounced mail, mail stuck for over 15 minutes, and AI drafts with the share that failed (error) and the share the model refused. An agency is flagged for any failed job or mail, any stuck mail, or an error or refusal share above 20% with at least 5 drafts. The latest 20 failed jobs are listed by id. Error text, subjects and addresses are never selected. | FR-ADM-09 asks for counts and ids only; the thresholds are mine. | `platform/health.ts`, `lib/db/job-runs.ts` |
| 35 | **Platform configuration** (T-16-02, FR-ADM-10): (1) the **AI routing table and prompt versions** are shown read only, because they live in code and a change is a deploy that needs a passing evaluation run (NFR-AI05), so no screen can change a model without one. (2) **"Default templates" (Q-15)** is read as the **settings new agencies start from** (matching weights, outreach timing, guard rules, rejection reasons, visibility, language): an owner or admin edits them with the same form an agency uses, and they apply to agencies created afterwards. The submission report and resume document templates stay in code, branded per agency (FR-TEN-04), because Q-15's answer is that Team of Wakers provides one default. (3) **Feature flags** per agency, two that exist today and are checked where the feature starts: `portals` (invitations, shares, portal sign-in and pages) and `email_sending` (sending from the product; copy and mark as sent keeps working). A flag is added only together with the code that honours it. Every change is in the platform audit log, and a flag change is also in the agency's activity log. | FR-ADM-10 names three things without defining them; these are the smallest readings that do something real. | `platform/config.ts`, `platform/features.ts` |
| 36 | **Billing** (T-13-08, ADR-013): the app talks to a `BillingClient` interface (live HTTP client, in-memory recording fake, or none). Usage goes out **daily**, not hourly as ADR-013 sketched, because the scheduler runs daily jobs; the AI cost is reported as growth since the last report, so nothing is lost. Seats are a daily snapshot metric and the plan's hard limit on `seats` is enforced locally on invitations and reactivations from the last synced state. The plan does not set the AI budget automatically. Moving an agency between plans is not done from here (the server call is not known); the screen says to do it in Billing Server. Details and what to provide: [billing.md](./billing.md). | The live wire format follows what product-manager already uses against the same server, but was not run against it. | `server/billing/` |
| 37 | The **client history and the pattern reports read only job-related fields**: the stages a candidate went through, the outreach answers, the hiring manager's ratings, and four candidate fields (level of the title, years of experience, skills, source). Never a name, contact detail, location, comment, or reason text. One loader does all the reading and a guard test (`facts.guard.test.ts`) fails if a forbidden field is ever selected there. | Brief 14, question 7 is about job-related characteristics only; NFR-AI04. Structural beats a filter. | `server/knowledge/facts.ts` |
| 38 | Any **value that names a protected characteristic is withheld** from a report, a history list and the facts of the summary, and counted ("1 value was left out"). The list covers age, gender, race and ethnicity, nationality, religion, disability and health, marital and family status and sexual orientation, in English and Korean, and errs towards leaving things out (for example "Asian markets" is withheld). | The values come from free text (skills, the agency's reason codes), which a recruiter may have typed anything into. | `core/knowledge/guardrails.ts` |
| 39 | **Small sample = fewer than 5 records.** Every row shows "n of m" and a "Few records" badge below that; a summary sentence that rests on such a fact is marked tentative. Years of experience are shown in four wide bands (0-2, 3-5, 6-10, 11+), not as numbers. | The brief asks for patterns that can be found again, not proof; wide bands do not single a person out. | `core/knowledge/guardrails.ts` |
| 40 | **Definitions used by the reports:** *put forward* = a submission exists or the candidate reached "submitted"; *interviewed* = reached "interviewing" or has a round that was not cancelled; *hired* = currently accepted or started (a candidate who reneged is not a hire); *quality candidate* (question 3) = the hiring manager wanted to interview them, counted out of those put forward; *response* (question 4) = the candidate answered the outreach, yes or no; *left* (question 5) = in a negative end state, at the stage they left from, with the reason code the recruiter recorded. | The brief names the questions but not the arithmetic. | `server/knowledge/facts.ts` |
| 41 | The reports show **only the positions the viewer can see** (the agency's visibility rule), for one client or for every client. Question 7 counts a match analysis only after the recruiter marked it reviewed. | FR-AI-02, ADR-006. | `positions/service.ts`, `knowledge/facts.ts` |
| 42 | **A18 gets numbered facts, not records.** The model sees counts and the labels of job-related groups, never a name, a comment or any free text from a record. It cites facts by id; code attaches the records behind each fact, so the links are always exact. | FR-KNW-03: every statement links to its records. | `ai/prompts/pattern-summarize.ts` |
| 43 | **The fact check of A18 is deterministic**, not the X1 model verifier: each sentence must cite real facts, every number in it must be one of the numbers of the facts it cites, and it must not mention a protected characteristic. A sentence that fails is removed (not repaired), and the draft says how many were removed. If nothing passes, no draft is saved. | The sources are structured counts, so a code check is stricter than a model. | `core/knowledge/summary-facts.ts` |
| 44 | **A18 is not streamed** to the screen. | A sentence that fails the check must never be shown, even for a moment. | `knowledge/summary.ts` |
| 45 | The A18 draft belongs to the **position** the recruiter asked from (artifact kind `pattern_summary`, subject position); it describes that position's client. | The kind was already registered for positions; no new subject type. | `core/artifacts/kinds.ts` |
| 46 | **The hiring manager's intake answers** wait in a `proposals` column on the intake record, one per topic, so an answer the recruiter already recorded is never overwritten. Accepting copies one into the intake as "from the hiring manager" and replaces that topic; the success profile (A5) uses accepted answers only. The questionnaire shows the recruiter's approved intake questions per topic (not the "gap in the JD" ones). | FR-HMP-03. | `positions/intake.ts`, `portal/publish.ts` |

## Running things

- **Database roles:** `pnpm db:setup` now also creates `easemuse_datarights`, the only login that can change audit rows. The worker needs `DATABASE_URL_DATARIGHTS` for candidate deletion (see `.env.example`); the job refuses to run without it.
- **AI evaluation:** `pnpm eval -- --suite all --max-cost 15` runs the labelled data in `eval-data/` against the real model (needs `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`; see `apps/web/evals/README.md`). Nothing has been run against a live model yet.
- **Print pages in a real browser:** see the header of `src/server/documents/render/live-render.db.test.ts`. The JD, resume and report pages have been printed with Chrome against a dev server and look right.
- **Not built:** SMTP transport (postponed on request), Korean translations (skeleton only, per ADR-015).
- **E2E (2026-10-08):** `pnpm --filter @easemuse/web exec playwright test` runs the browser suite, including the release 1 happy path, on its own server (port 3100, `easemuse_test`, Redis db 1, `AI_FAKE=on`). Stop any other `next dev` of this app first; the test database must be migrated (global setup does it).
- **Keys for pool search and the benchmark (2026-10-07):** the Voyage key (`pa-...`) is entered by an owner in `/admin/settings` or set as `VOYAGE_API_KEY`; without it pool search uses keywords and new profiles are not indexed (the nightly catch-up indexes them once a key exists). Web search needs `WEB_SEARCH=on` **and** web search switched on for the Anthropic account in the Claude Console; the key also needs credit. Nothing in this area has been run against a live provider: Voyage and web search are covered by fakes, and pgvector by the real database.
